# Worked Project 1 — "Second Shift" (Documentary Segment)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate an integrated, truthful documentary workflow with limited coverage, imperfect location sound, ethically faithful compression, restrained graphics and grade, accessible bilingual captions, and a late ethical change — showing how each department's decisions depend on the others.

**Status:** hypothetical. No footage was analysed; all observations, times and values are illustrative and labelled as such. Fixed assumptions are in [Decision Method](../FOUNDATIONS/Decision-Method.md#project-a--second-shift-documentary-segment).

**Frame rate and timecode:** 25 fps; master timeline starts 01:00:00:00; times below are given as programme time (mm:ss) for readability.

---

## 1. Brief and assumptions

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| One-sentence intent | Viewers come away seeing Asha as a skilled professional whose night work is mostly invisible, and understanding that night care depends on a few overstretched people — without pity framing |
| Audience | General public and the nonprofit's partners; most have never been inside a district hospital at night |
| Language | Hindi interview and actuality; Hindi closed captions; English subtitles |
| Viewing contexts | Online series page (laptop/phone, usually sound on); 60-second social excerpt (muted start); community screenings with projector and small PA |
| Desired change | Viewers can name one specific thing Asha does that they did not know about; partners use the film in funding conversations |
| Truths to protect | Patients unidentifiable; events in true order unless signposted; no reconstruction; Asha's meaning preserved in translation |
| Emotional stance | Respectful closeness; quiet; unhurried |
| Duration | 6:00 (±10 s) |
| Resources | Two-person crew for one interview day and one night; one editor (edit, grade, mix); remote translator and caption editor; two-week post |
| Approvals | Director (editorial), nonprofit communications lead (organisational), hospital (patient privacy only), Asha (representation review of key passages) |
| Success indicators | Fresh-viewer descriptions; partner feedback; no privacy complaints |

**What would change the approach (hinge variables):** if Asha asked for no social excerpt, the 9:16 version would be dropped; if the hospital refused night filming, the film would become an interview-led portrait with day exteriors and archive; if the nonprofit insisted on a direct appeal from Asha, the director would propose the end-card alternative described in [Creative Brief](../FOUNDATIONS/Creative-Brief.md#resolving-conflicts-between-layers).

## 4. Testimony strategy

Developed in [Interview Craft](../STORY/Interview-Craft.md#12-worked-example-2--a-nervous-first-time-subject-hypothetical-project-a-second-shift):
- Listening plan: five needed answers (typical night as sequence; one patient moment without identification; invisible work; effect outside work; why she continues).
- Sensitive areas agreed in advance: staffing and management (handled carefully, not pursued); family only if she raises it.
- Conduct: chronology before interpretation; patient silence; "anything I should have asked?" at the end — which produced the daughter story.
- Live log: four hero moments with timecode, including the 4-second pause.

## 6. Cut candidates

### 6.1 Opening (00:00–00:35)

| Candidate | Description | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| A. Cold open on the emergency | Dramatic hook | **Rejected:** breaches the observational contract by front-loading crisis out of context; frames the film as a thriller |
| B. Establishing exterior at dusk, then interview | Conventional orientation | Credible, but slow and generic |
| C. Black screen with Asha's voice telling the daughter story, then her face | Intimate, specific, honest | **Chosen:** creates a genuine question ("why does she sleep in the day?") answered within 40 seconds |

### 6.2 The reflection (≈04:05–04:45)

Compression of the interview passage follows [Performance Editing](../EDITING/Performance-Editing.md#8-worked-example-2--interview-cut-with-missing-coverage-hypothetical-project-a): visible jump cut instead of covering B-roll, because B-cam coverage is missing and generic ward B-roll would imply false connections. The 4-second pause is kept whole.

### 6.3 Credible rejected alternative — music-led ending

A version with a sparse piano cue under the whole reflection and dawn was cut and tested. It was rejected because fresh viewers described feeling "told to be sad" more often than with the no-music reflection (see [Music Selection](../AUDIO/Music-Selection.md#7-worked-example-2--a-reflective-documentary-scene-hypothetical-project-a-second-shift)). The chosen version keeps the reflection in room tone and introduces the cue at dawn.

## 7. Pacing, attention, geography and camera

- **Pacing:** the [intensity map](../EDITING/Rhythm-and-Pacing.md#filled-map-1--project-a-second-shift-hypothetical-25-fps-times-illustrative) shows one peak (emergency) and a low-density, high-intensity reflection.
- **Attention:** hands first, then faces; patients kept out of focus or out of frame; the brightest element in night shots is usually the desk lamp near Asha.
- **Geography:** a wide of the ward early (beat 3) establishes where the nurses' station and beds are; the handheld follows later cut within that map.
- **Camera:** handheld behind and beside Asha at walking pace; locked-off for reflective moments (see [Motivated Movement](../CAMERA/Motivated-Movement.md#8-worked-example-2--a-documentary-handheld-follow-hypothetical-project-a-second-shift-25-fps)).

## 8. Lighting, acquisition and correction strategy

- **Interview:** window key, short-side, negative fill, overheads off (see [Practical Setups](../LIGHTING/Practical-Setups.md#2-worked-example-1--window-lit-interview-hypothetical-project-a-second-shift)).
- **Night ward:** no added light (crew decision for patient comfort); 1/50 s shutter avoids mains flicker from fluorescent tubes (see [Capture Decisions](../CAMERA/Capture-Decisions.md#6-light-flicker)); exposure protects faces; noisy shadows accepted.
- **Limits accepted:** some clipped window highlights in the interview; noise in night shadows; the B-cam's processed look — handled in correction, not hidden.

## 9. Colour pipeline, look and repair limits

- **Pipeline:** simple managed workflow — A-cam log input transform → working space → BT.709/BT.1886 output; phone treated as display-referred (see [Color Management](../COLOR/Color-Management.md#4-a-simple-colour-managed-workflow-hypothetical-project-a-second-shift)).
- **Look:** restrained, naturalistic (see [Creative Grading](../COLOR/Creative-Grading.md#6-worked-example-1--a-restrained-documentary-grade-hypothetical-project-a-second-shift)).
- **Matching:** B-cam matched to A-cam; texture unified with fine grain (see [Correction and Shot Matching](../COLOR/Correction-and-Shot-Matching.md#6-worked-example-1--mixed-camera-interview-match-hypothetical-project-a-25-fps)).
- **Repair limits:** clipped window cannot gain detail (reduced in brightness, not "recovered"); noisy night shadows denoised moderately — skin texture preserved over cleanliness.

## 16. Late change — the emergency scene is withdrawn

**Change (after picture lock, grade and mix):** the family of the patient in the emergency scene learns about the film through hospital staff. Although the patient is not identifiable, they are uncomfortable. The hospital passes on the concern; the nonprofit and director decide to honour it and remove the observational emergency scene (03:20–04:05, 45 s).

### Change-impact map

| Area | Effect | Action | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story | The film loses its escalation; the reflection that follows loses weight (pacing is relational; see [Rhythm and Pacing](../EDITING/Rhythm-and-Pacing.md#changing-a-single-beat-changes-everything-after-it)) | Use Asha's own interview account of an emergency night ("his breathing changed. You learn to hear that…" — the compressed passage from [Performance Editing](../EDITING/Performance-Editing.md#8-worked-example-2--interview-cut-with-missing-coverage-hypothetical-project-a)) over a static shot of the empty corridor at 3 a.m. (no patients) | Fresh viewers still sense the night's stakes |

## 17. Integrated result

**What was sacrificed and why:**
- **Observational intensity** in the emergency — sacrificed to respect a family's wishes; the film's truth is preserved through Asha's own account.
- **Music under the reflection** — sacrificed for honesty of feeling.
- **Fluent retellings** — sacrificed for the truthful, hesitant originals.
- **Cosmetic cleanliness** (noise, clipped windows) — accepted in favour of presence.

**Disconfirming test:** if fresh viewers of the revised film describe Asha with pity words ("poor", "sad", "victim") more than with skill words ("capable", "calm", "experienced"), the film's approach has failed its intent, and the restraint choices (no music, long pause) should be re-examined — perhaps the reflection needs more context or the competence beat needs strengthening.
