# Worked Projects: Overview

**Purpose:** Explain how the four integrated worked projects demonstrate the interaction between disciplines — how a decision in one department creates obligations and consequences in others — and how to read them.

**Status of all material:** every project is **hypothetical**. No footage was analysed. Assumptions are fixed in [Decision Method](../FOUNDATIONS/Decision-Method.md#7-recurring-hypothetical-projects) and developed here. All times, levels and values are `[Illustrative]` unless labelled as verified specifications.

---

## 1. The four projects

| # | Project | Form | Frame rate | What it demonstrates most |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Second Shift](01-Documentary-Second-Shift.md) | 6-minute documentary segment | 25 fps | Truthful testimony with limited coverage and imperfect sound; ethical compression; restraint in music, graphics and grade; accessible bilingual text; a late ethical change |
| 2 | [Arc](02-Product-Brand-Arc.md) | 30 s product spot, 90 s brand film, vertical and bumper versions | 23.976 fps | Clear audience and action; product legibility; considered camera and motion grammar; music and sound emphasis; graphic hierarchy; horizontal/vertical adaptation; a late duration change |
| 3 | [Clear Water](03-Explainer-Clear-Water.md) | 5-minute bilingual explainer | 29.97 fps NDF | Factual clarity; demonstration evidence; information pacing; honest charts; spoken explanation; readable captions in two scripts; a late factual change |
| 4 | [The Spare Key](04-Narrative-Scene-Spare-Key.md) | 3-minute narrative scene | 23.976 fps | Performance selection; geography and eye trace; practical lighting; silence; a late structural change (dramatic irony) |

## 2. How each project document is organised

Every project follows the same sequence so that projects can be compared:

1. Brief and assumptions, including what would change the approach.
2. Material inventory, missing coverage, feasibility and rights.
3. Story/beat map with viewer knowledge and emotion.
4. Testimony or source strategy (where relevant).
5. Selects and performance strategy; scene/sequence map.
6. Cut candidates, including a credible rejected alternative.
7. Pacing, attention, geography and camera.
8. Lighting, acquisition and correction strategy.
9. Colour pipeline, look and repair limits.
10. Motion, type and caption plan.
11. Dialogue, music, sound and AI-audio plan, including a restraint choice.
12. VFX and AI decisions with controls and rejection conditions.
13. Version matrix and delivery verification.
14. Review notes, diagnosis, repair, dependent changes and acceptance.
15. Micro-timeline table.
16. A late change and its dependency effects.
17. Integrated result: what was sacrificed, and a test that could disconfirm the approach.

## 3. Cross-project lessons

| Lesson | Where it shows |
|---|---|
| The same technique is right in one contract and wrong in another (music under testimony; speed ramps; overshoot) | Projects 1 vs 2 |
| Front-loading versus withholding information depends on the form | Projects 3 vs 4 |
| Restraint is a decision with consequences, not an absence of decisions | Projects 1 and 4 (silence), 2 (motion), 3 (no generated imagery) |
| Late changes are dependency problems | Every project's Section 16 |
| Accessibility shapes edit and design, not just delivery | Projects 1 and 3 |
| Vertical versions are new edits | Projects 1 and 2 |

## 4. How to use them

- **To learn:** read a craft chapter, then find its decisions applied in a project (each project section links to the canonical chapter).
- **To plan a real project:** copy the section structure; replace hypothetical facts with real ones; keep the evidence labels honest (see [Decision Method](../FOUNDATIONS/Decision-Method.md#5-evidence-categories-when-discussing-material)).
- **To teach:** use the late-change sections as exercises — ask students to predict dependent changes before reading them.
