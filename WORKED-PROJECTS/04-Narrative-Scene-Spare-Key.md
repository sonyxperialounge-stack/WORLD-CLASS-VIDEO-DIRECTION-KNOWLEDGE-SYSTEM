# Worked Project 4 — "The Spare Key" (Narrative Scene)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate an integrated drama workflow for a single dialogue scene: performance selection, continuity and geography, eye trace, motivated stillness and movement, practical lighting, a preserved mixed-temperature grade, silence as design, subtitles for mixed-language dialogue, and a late structural change that converts surprise into dramatic irony.

**Status:** hypothetical. Fixed assumptions are in [Decision Method](../FOUNDATIONS/Decision-Method.md#project-d--the-spare-key-narrative-scene). All times illustrative.

**Frame rate and timecode:** 23.976 fps, non-drop-frame; shutter changed on set from 1/48 s to 1/50 s for flicker safety under LED practicals (see [Capture Decisions](../CAMERA/Capture-Decisions.md#9-worked-example-1--led-practical-flicker-hypothetical-project-d-23976-fps-in-a-50-hz-region)). Times below are scene time (m:ss).

---

## 1. Brief and assumptions

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Intent | The audience experiences Nisha's re-evaluation of Ravi at the moment the spare key is revealed; the reversal reframes the entire argument |
| Audience | Festival audiences; online festival screeners watched on laptops and phones |
| Language | Hindi-English mixed dialogue; English subtitles |
| Contract | Fiction; emotionally truthful; internal consistency |
| Emotional stance | Melancholy, restrained; no melodrama |
| Duration | ≈3:00 |
| Resources | One night; small crew; three to six takes per setup |
| Deliverables | Festival master (16:9 delivery with 2.39:1 framing); English subtitle sidecar; burned-in subtitle screener |
| Approvals | Director, producer |

**What would change the approach:** if the actors' strongest takes were in the master rather than singles, the scene would be cut wider with fewer cuts; if the festival required a DCP, a specialist conversion with a Scope container would be needed (out of this project's scope); if test audiences found the reveal confusing, the setup would be strengthened (as happens in Section 16).

## 4. Performance capture strategy

(In place of a testimony strategy.) Rehearsal focused on intentions rather than line readings: Nisha wants closure; Ravi wants to protect a promise without explaining it. The director asked Ravi's actor to touch the pocket only when Nisha says something about their father — giving the editor motivated inserts. Coverage was planned by beats (see [Composition and Optics](../CAMERA/Composition-and-Optics.md#shot-lists-based-on-story-beats-not-shot-sizes)).

## 5. Selects and performance strategy

From [Performance Editing](../EDITING/Performance-Editing.md#7-worked-example-1--two-person-scene-hypothetical-project-d-the-spare-key-23976-fps):
- Nisha: T3 first half, T5 from the escalation (join at her turn to the box).
- Ravi: T4 listening first half; T5 for the reveal.
- Reactions are taken from the takes where they were genuinely performed against the other actor's lines (fiction permits reaction portability, but the strongest reactions are the real ones).

## 7. Pacing, attention, geography and camera

- **Pacing:** the 40 seconds before the reveal hold long shots with internal tension (see [Rhythm and Pacing](../EDITING/Rhythm-and-Pacing.md#8-worked-example-1--a-slow-looking-sequence-with-high-internal-tension-hypothetical-project-d)).
- **Attention:** attention map around the reveal (see [Continuity and Attention](../EDITING/Continuity-and-Attention.md#filled-attention-map--project-d-key-reveal-hypothetical-inferred-focal-points-frame-divided-into-a-33-grid-lcr--topmiddlebottom)); the lamp kept below Nisha's face in brightness in the re-establishing wide.
- **Geography:** established by the opening wide from the doorway (Nisha's entry), reset by Ravi's walk.
- **Camera:** static throughout the argument; the first camera movement is a slow push-in on Nisha after the key lands — its rarity gives it meaning (reference function in [Reference Analysis](../FOUNDATIONS/Reference-Analysis.md#61-labelled-moodboards)).

## 8. Lighting and acquisition

Practical-driven night interior with hidden supplementary sources motivated by the lamps; cool streetlight accent; ceiling light off; flicker-free fixtures; 1/50 s shutter (see [Practical Setups](../LIGHTING/Practical-Setups.md#3-worked-example-2--night-interior-with-practical-fixtures-hypothetical-project-d-the-spare-key-23976-fps-150-s)). One limitation: in Nisha's T5 single, the supplementary source was too far forward, weakening the lamp's direction.

## 9. Colour pipeline, look and repair limits

- **Pipeline:** camera log → working space → BT.709 output; 2.39:1 framing within a 16:9 delivery frame (letterbox); framing guides used on set.
- **Correction:** the mixed-temperature scene preserved — warm skin under lamps, cool window — rather than neutralised (see [Correction and Shot Matching](../COLOR/Correction-and-Shot-Matching.md#7-worked-example-2--a-deliberately-mixed-temperature-scene-hypothetical-project-d)).
- **Look:** pools of warm light, dark (not crushed) space between the siblings; fine grain.
- **Relighting:** subtle tracked shaping on Nisha's T5 to strengthen the lamp direction; no new shadows invented (see [Texture and Post Relighting](../COLOR/Texture-and-Post-Relighting.md#8-worked-example-2--strengthening-motivated-light-hypothetical-project-d)).
- **Repair limit:** when Nisha turns away from the lamp, the shaping is keyframed off because it would contradict real light.

## 10. Motion, type and subtitle plan

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Titles | Single title card at the head of the short (outside this scene) |
| Subtitles | English subtitles for Hindi and mixed lines; English lines in the original language are not subtitled (or are, for accessibility, in an SDH track); speaker identification by position and dashes |
| Subtitle placement | Sidecar version: within the active picture near the bottom edge (players may display it differently); burned-in festival screener: in the lower letterbox bar, after confirming the festival projects the full 16:9 frame — if a venue masks to 2.39, the bar subtitles would be lost, so the confirmation is a delivery requirement |
| Reading checks | Two-line maximum; rates checked against the festival's subtitle guidance if provided; the whispered "When?" subtitled as a single word, timed exactly to the whisper |
| Translation | Faithful; politeness shifts (आप to तुम when Nisha softens) conveyed through word choice where possible (see [Localization](../TEXT-ACCESSIBILITY/Localization.md#2-faithful-meaning-versus-literal-words)) |

## 14. Review notes, diagnosis, repair and acceptance

| Note | Diagnosis | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| "Is someone else in the room?" | Axis crossing in OTS shots | OTS only after Ravi's walk |
| "The key reveal doesn't land" | Pocket setup too short; composed reaction take; music swell pre-empting emotion (see [Failure Modes and Repair](../DIAGNOSTICS/Failure-Modes-and-Repair.md#5-worked-example-2--an-emotionally-flat-scene-hypothetical-project-d)) | Longer pocket inserts; T5 reaction; swell removed |
| "Window looks odd at 0:40" | Crew reflection | Cleanup |
| "Subtitles cover Nisha's mouth in the close-up" | Sidecar placement in active image during a low-framed close-up | Raise for those events (SDH) or rely on letterbox in burned-in version |

**Final acceptance criteria:** (1) fresh viewers describe Nisha's change of view in their own words; (2) nobody reports geography confusion; (3) nothing in the sound reads as a dropout; (4) subtitles readable on a phone screener; (5) QC passed.

## 16. Late change — from surprise to dramatic irony

**Change:** at a test screening, half the audience misses the significance of the key at the reveal (they didn't register the pocket business). The director decides to **show the audience the key itself** early — Ravi's hand closing around it in his pocket at about 0:55 — so the argument becomes painful to watch rather than a surprise (see [Story Architecture](../STORY/Story-Architecture.md#8-negative-space-inference-irony-and-reveals)).

### Change-impact map

| Area | Effect | Action | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story | Viewer now knows more than Nisha; tension replaces surprise; the reveal becomes Nisha's discovery rather than ours | Restructure the "Ravi resists" beat around the insert | Test audience describes tension ("I wanted him to tell her") |
| Cuts | A new insert of the key in Ravi's hand (from the insert setup's alternate take) at ≈0:55; existing pocket touches retained | Edit; total length +2 s | Full-speed review |
| Performance | Ravi's later hesitations now read as withholding — some trims to avoid over-signalling | Shorten one hesitation | Fresh-viewer check for "too obvious" |
| Pacing | The reveal can be slightly quicker (we know); the silence after it now belongs to Nisha's discovery | Shorten the reduced-sound section by ≈1 s | Audio-only pass |
| Sound | A faint key jingle at the new insert, then a gentle hum lift; the reveal's traffic fade remains | Sound design | Sync check |
| Music | None (unchanged) | — | — |
| Subtitles | Timings after 0:55 shift by +2 s | Re-time sidecar and burned-in screener | Player check |
| Grade | New insert graded to match | Grade | Shot comparison |
| VFX | Window cleanup shot shifts in time but is unchanged | Re-conform | Conform check |
| Deliverables | New festival master and screener versions; old screener links disabled | Version update | Matrix reconciled |
| Approvals | Director and producer | Review | Signed |

## 17. Integrated result

**What was sacrificed and why:**
- **Perfect continuity** (photo frame) — for the stronger take 5.
- **Score** — for silence and the scene's own sounds.
- **Camera movement** — withheld until the reveal so that the one move means something.
- **Surprise** (after the late change) — traded for dramatic irony, because the test showed the surprise was not landing for half the audience.

**Disconfirming test:** if, in the revised version, test audiences describe the scene as "predictable" or "obvious" and report *less* emotional engagement than with the original, the dramatic-irony choice is wrong for this scene; the alternative repair would be to keep the surprise but strengthen the earlier pocket setups only.
