# Constraints, Resources and Priorities

**Purpose:** Teach how to make resource decisions that protect the brief — what to spend on, what to sacrifice, and how to change course coherently when the objective changes mid-project.

**Applies to:** Pre-production planning, schedule crises, scope negotiations and any late change.

**Prerequisites:** [Creative Brief](Creative-Brief.md), [Decision Method](Decision-Method.md).

**Related owners:** Scheduling and handoffs in [Production and Handoffs](../OPERATIONS/Production-and-Handoffs.md); review rounds and change logging in [Review and Revisions](../OPERATIONS/Review-and-Revisions.md).

---

## 1. Constraints are design inputs, not excuses

Every project runs out of something: days, money, access, coverage, crew skill, rights, review time. Inexperienced teams spread scarcity evenly and end with a piece that is a little weak everywhere. Experienced teams **concentrate resources where the brief is decided** and accept visible simplicity elsewhere.

The guiding question `[Heuristic]`: *Which part of this piece, if weak, makes the whole piece fail its intent?* Spend there first.

| Project | The part that decides success | What can be simpler |
|---|---|---|
| A "Second Shift" | Asha's interview performance and the truth of the night footage | Graphics (a name card and two location titles), grade (correction plus restraint) |
| B "Arc" 30 s | The product reveal and the lid ring's legibility | Founder material is not in the 30 s spot; background environments can be minimal |
| C "Clear Water" | The clarity of the mechanism and the maintenance steps | Cinematic camera movement; elaborate music |
| D "The Spare Key" | The two performances and the key reveal | Establishing exteriors; production design beyond the flat's living room |

## 2. Classic resource trade-offs

### 2.1 Small crew versus controlled set

| Option | Gains | Costs | Choose when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small mobile crew (1–3 people) | Access, intimacy, lower intrusion, adaptability, more shooting days for the money | Less lighting control, compromised sound, single-camera coverage, operator fatigue | Observational documentary, sensitive subjects, real locations, unpredictable events |
| Controlled set (studio or locked location, larger crew) | Repeatability, lighting precision, clean sound, product handling, multiple takes | Cost per day, less spontaneity, possible stiffness in non-actors | Product photography, scripted scenes, demonstrations that must be exactly repeatable |

**Hypothetical decision (Project A):** a two-person crew (director-camera plus sound recordist) is chosen for the night ward. A larger crew would bring lights that change the ward's atmosphere and disturb patients. The consequence — fluorescent flicker risk, high noise floor, single-camera coverage — is accepted and planned for: a flicker-safe shutter check on arrival (see [Capture Decisions](../CAMERA/Capture-Decisions.md)), room tone recorded in every space, and a story structure that does not require matched coverage.

### 2.2 Scarce footage versus heavy design

When footage is thin, two directions are possible: build the piece from design (graphics, animation, text), or make the thin footage carry more by structure and sound.

| Option | When it helps | When it hurts |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy design (motion graphics, illustration, archival montage) | The subject is abstract (a process, data, history) and the footage cannot show it | Testimony and human presence get buried; design becomes decoration to fill time; the budget moves to the least credible element |
| Structural economy (fewer, longer shots; sound-led scenes; voice carries transitions) | Strong performance or atmosphere exists but in small quantities | The audience lacks visual information it genuinely needs |
| Targeted pickup shoot | A specific missing shot blocks comprehension | Time and access cost; continuity risk |

**Hypothetical decision (Project C):** the demonstration cannot be reshot, and two maintenance steps (scrubbing the candle, reassembling the seal) were filmed only in one wide shot. Animating the whole explainer would cost the trust that real demonstration provides. Choice: keep the real demonstration as the backbone; add two short animated inserts only for the steps the wide shot cannot show, using the same object proportions and colours so viewers connect them to the real filter. See [Integration and Data Graphics](../MOTION-DESIGN/Integration-and-Data-Graphics.md).

### 2.3 Fast delivery versus complex finishing

| Option | Gains | Costs | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-operator, single-application finish (editor grades and mixes in the NLE) | Speed; no interchange; fewer handoff errors | Less specialist depth; monitoring limits | Keep the look simple; spend the saved time on dialogue clarity and caption accuracy |
| Specialist finishing (colorist, mixer, online editor) | Quality, independent eyes, better monitoring | Handoff time; interchange failures; scheduling | Lock picture properly; prepare clean turnovers; budget for one conform check — see [Production and Handoffs](../OPERATIONS/Production-and-Handoffs.md) |

A practical warning `[Practice]`: compressing the finishing schedule rarely removes work; it moves defects to the audience. If time must be cut, cut breadth (fewer versions, fewer graphic flourishes) before cutting quality-control passes.

## 3. The priority map

A priority map is a list of interventions classified by their contribution to the brief, with the consequence of dropping each one stated explicitly. It is made before the shoot and revised at each milestone.

### Filled example — Project B "Arc" (hypothetical)

| Intervention | Class | Why | Consequence if dropped |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean product reveal with legible lid ring at 30 s and 15 s lengths | Must-have | It is the proof point | Spot fails its intent |
| Approved app UI on the phone screen | Must-have | Legal and accuracy | Cannot release |
| Captions on all versions | Must-have | Muted viewing; accessibility | Inaccessible; lost message in feed |
| 9:16 version recomposed, not cropped | Must-have | Vertical is a primary destination | Crop removes the ring or the hand action |
| High-frame-rate pour shot for slow motion | Useful | Adds sensory appeal to the "drink" beat | Real-time pour is acceptable; slightly less sensory |
| Custom sound design for the ring activation | Useful | Gives the ring an identity | Library sound with editing is adequate |
| Founder cameo in 30 s spot | Expendable | Brand film covers the founder | None for the spot's intent |
| 3D animated exploded view of the lid | Expendable | Impressive but not needed to understand the benefit | Saves about 4 motion-design days |

The map's power is in the last column. When the schedule slips, the team does not argue in the abstract; it drops from the bottom and reads the consequence aloud.

### Filled example — Project A "Second Shift" (hypothetical, abbreviated)

| Intervention | Class | Consequence if dropped |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue cleanup to intelligibility on interview and key ward moments | Must-have | Captions would have to carry meaning the audience cannot hear; Hindi speakers lose nuance |
| Accurate Hindi captions and faithful English subtitles | Must-have | Inaccessible; meaning distorted for English viewers |
| Face blurring of identifiable patients without consent | Must-have | Legal and ethical breach |
| Shot matching between A-cam and phone B-cam | Useful | Visible jumps in colour; distracting but not misleading |
| Score | Expendable (possibly undesirable) | None; the piece may be stronger without it |
| Animated map of the district | Expendable | A simple location title suffices |

## 4. Resource-aware advice in every chapter

Each craft chapter in this library includes low-resource options. The general principle `[Heuristic]`: **placement and timing before equipment.** Moving the subject relative to a window, changing the interview time, choosing a quieter room, or shooting the product on a turntable at a slower speed usually gives more improvement per unit of money than any purchase. Equipment matters when placement is exhausted.

| Problem | Cheap first move | Expensive move worth considering only after |
|---|---|---|
| Flat interview image | Turn the subject so the window is to one side; add black fabric on the other side (negative fill) | Additional fixtures |
| Noisy interview | Choose the quietest room; turn off fans and fridges with permission; place the mic closer | Restoration software or ADR |
| Shaky handheld | Wider lens, slower walking, brace against walls, shorter shots | Gimbal |
| Boring product footage | Plan the reveal order; control reflections with large bounce or black cards | Motion-control rig |
| Weak graphics | Reduce elements; strengthen typography hierarchy; design static frames first | More animation |

## 5. The brief-change process

Briefs change. A funder wants a different emphasis, legal removes a claim, a platform version is added, the duration is halved. A late change is dangerous not because it is large but because its **dependencies are invisible**: a duration change affects music phrases, caption cues, graphic timings, the grade's shot list and the mix.

### The process

1. **Restate the change as an intent change.** "Make it 60 seconds" is a duration change; ask what intent must survive. "The 60-second version must still let a viewer state what Asha does and why it matters."
2. **Classify the change.** Is it a new objective, a new audience, a new constraint, or a preference? New objectives re-open story; constraints usually re-open only execution.
3. **Map the impact** using the change-impact map below.
4. **Price it** in time and risk, and identify what drops from the priority map to pay for it.
5. **Get approval from the owner of the objective,** not only from the person who requested the change.
6. **Update the brief version,** then propagate changes in dependency order: story → picture edit → music edit → sound → graphics and captions → grade/VFX conform → versions → QC.
7. **Re-verify** the acceptance checks of every affected department; do not assume unaffected departments are unaffected.

### Change-impact map — filled example (hypothetical, Project C "Clear Water")

**Change:** after picture lock, the public-health advisor revises one factual line: "removes 99% of bacteria" becomes "reduces most bacteria when the candle is maintained; it does not remove viruses — boil water if unsure."

| Department | Impact | Action | Owner | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Script / VO | Line becomes longer; adds a safety instruction | Rewrite both language versions for speakability; re-record two VO lines in each language | Writer, narrator | Advisor approves exact wording in both languages |
| Picture edit | The demonstration shot under the line must extend by about 2.5 s (illustrative) | Extend the shot from handles; add a cutaway of the boiling pot from existing B-roll | Editor | Full-speed playback of the section; no jump |
| Graphics | The bar chart claimed "99%"; must now show "reduction" without a single headline number | Redesign the chart annotation; remove the animated "99%" counter | Motion designer | Chart values match the advisor's source; no implied precision |
| Music | Extension falls mid-phrase | Extend by repeating a bar from the stem; check the ending still lands | Editor / composer | Listen through the join on speakers and headphones |
| Captions | New text, new timing in both languages | Re-time cues; check reading rate | Caption editor | Captions checked on the encoded file |
| Mix | New VO lines; different room match | Match EQ/level to existing VO; re-check loudness | Mixer | Integrated loudness re-measured to the delivery target |
| Grade / VFX | New cutaway needs grading | Grade the inserted shot to match | Colorist | Shot-to-shot comparison |
| Versions | Both language versions and the 60-second social cut | Update the version matrix; mark old exports superseded | Producer | Version matrix shows new parent master |
| QC | All affected versions | Re-run text, sound, export and playback checks | QC | Signed QC sheet per version |

This example is developed further in the [Explainer worked project](../WORKED-PROJECTS/03-Explainer-Clear-Water.md).

## 6. Counterexample and repair: even scarcity

**Hypothetical counterexample.** A small team making a 3-minute nonprofit film divides its budget evenly: a day of drone footage, a motion designer for an animated logo, a licensed epic track and a colorist. The interview is shot in a reverberant hall with a camera-mounted microphone because the sound recordist was cut from the budget. The finished film looks expensive and sounds hollow; viewers at a screening strain to understand the main speaker and remember the drone shots.

**Diagnosis.** The priority map was never made. The element that decided success — intelligible, credible testimony — received the least investment.

**Repair.** For the next film, the team writes the priority map first: sound recordist and a quiet location become must-haves; the drone and animated logo become expendable. On the current film, the available repair is limited: dialogue restoration can reduce reverberation somewhat, but cannot recover clarity never captured (see [Recording and Cleanup](../AUDIO/Recording-and-Cleanup.md)). The team re-records a short clarifying voice-over from the speaker in a quiet room, disclosed as a later recording if the documentary contract requires it, and accurate captions carry the remaining hard-to-hear lines.

**Verification.** At the next screening, ask viewers to repeat the speaker's main point. If they can, intelligibility is adequate for the intent.

## 7. Micro-decisions

- Put the priority map on one page; if it needs two pages, it is not prioritised.
- Assign a named person to defend each must-have.
- Price changes in days and in what is dropped, not only in money.
- When cutting scope, remove whole elements rather than degrading many; a missing flourish is invisible, a rushed one is not.
- Keep a "parking lot" of good ideas that were dropped, so they are not repeatedly re-proposed in reviews.

## 8. Failure signatures and corrections

| Symptom | Likely cause | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| The piece looks expensive in places and weak where it matters | Resources spread evenly; no priority map | Identify the success-deciding element; re-allocate; drop expendables |
| Schedule slips cut QC passes | Breadth protected over quality | Cut versions or flourishes before cutting checks |
| A late change breaks departments nobody consulted | Dependencies invisible | Change-impact map; dependency-ordered propagation |
| The same dropped idea keeps returning in reviews | No record of why it was dropped | Parking-lot list with reasons |
| Equipment purchases don't improve results | Placement and timing not exhausted first | Cheap first moves (position, time, room) before equipment |

## 9. Verification

- Can every team member name the part of the piece that decides success?
- Does the priority map state consequences, not only rankings?
- After a change, does every affected department have a re-verification entry?
- Was the change approved by the owner of the objective?
